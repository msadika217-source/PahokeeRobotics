Bender Bots is a team that has accomplished many things over the past years from several groups. Our team has brought out the best of our members capabilities and creativity to innovate while giving back to our community.

Team Intel Profile

Established in Pahokee, Florida, Bender Bots team 16724 consists of an even bigger group than the previous year with 15 active members. Our team consists of new members with it being 13 freshmen and 2 juniors. Some of our current team members have been in robotics in the past while others are brand new learning members. Our team strives to embark on a new journey within the world of robotics.

Everyone plays a role in building the team that we are, from a captain, co-captain, builders, coders, to the social media team, notebook, and artists. Our team goal is to represent Pahokee High School as a school that not only excels through sports but also through STEM and academics.

Robot Name: "Bendy"

We decided to name our robot “Bendy”. We wanted a name that would connect all of us through the robot, while keeping it fun and admirable. Our team is always going through challenges while bending the limits of innovation. As a team, Bendy has become a part of our robotics family.

ENGINEERING MODIFICATIONS:

Our final robot has the same nature as our last mechanisms, such as the intake/outtake, but we decided on torching down the metal sheet to our incline supporter because before, the sheet would lift just enough that artifacts got stuck. We reduced spacing in the intake wheels for maximum rubber band grip. Bendy is now fully optimized to store 3 artifacts simultaneously instead of 2.

⭐ SIGNATURE ABILITY: SHARP SHOOTING

When Bendy is in the zone there is no stopping it—it makes sharp and consistent shooting tracking. Features a lucky blue star sticker mounted on the top outtake control motor to commemorate our journey.

Campaign Journey & Strategy Obstacles

As newcomers to FTC, we encountered a heavy learning curve with advanced codebase logic, battery resource draining, and mechanical path blocks. We solved the power leaks by restructuring code routines and securing funds via the outreach squad.

When the intake channel jammed during Match #3, our hardware group diagnosed an elevated launcher plate profile. Because the high-strength metal layout resisted standard forming tools, the team applied thermal optimization (torching/melting elements slightly) to safely realign the geometry before Regional qualifications.

Outside the engineering grid, when a corporate partner ghosted us mid-campaign, we preserved our fighting spirit, re-routed our schedule, and locked in a successful local business fundraiser instead.
